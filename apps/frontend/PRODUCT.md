# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users
Invited people who are viewing flats to buy. They use the app **on site, phone in hand**: walking through the flat, often next to the agent, tapping answers quickly and often with one hand. Reviewing saved checks later at a desk is secondary. The audience is generic and international: not tied to one country, currency or legal system.

## Product Purpose
Tiny Flat Checker helps buyers inspect a flat's real condition by offering checks they would never remember on their own: damp behind furniture, water pressure, pending building works, missing documents. Success means a buyer leaves the viewing with every check answered and a record they can compare or share.

## Positioning
One curated, versioned checklist that is the same for everyone. It is not a blank notes app, and it is not a listing portal.

## Operating Context
- Used during a viewing that lasts 15–40 minutes. Signal can be patchy, attention is split, and lighting varies.
- Anyone can fill in the checklist on the landing page without an account and download it as a PDF.
- Invited users sign in with Google to save checks to their profile, then reopen, download or delete them.

## Capabilities and Constraints
- The checklist template is versioned. Each saved check records its template version and must render with that version.
- There are four field types: yes/no, a rating from 1 to 5, a single choice from options, and free text.
- Flat metadata is a title, an optional listing link and an asking price. No currency is stored.
- Access is invite-only, with Google as the only login. Saving requires login; PDF download works for everyone and for every saved check.
- A draft must survive the sign-in redirect.
- The copy is in English.

## Brand Commitments
The name is "Tiny Flat Checker". There are no other assets, and the voice is open.

## Evidence on Hand
There are no testimonials, user counts or press. Don't fabricate any.

## Product Principles
1. The checklist comes first, on the very first screen. Nothing stands between a visitor and the first check.
2. It must be usable while walking: large targets, one-thumb reach, answers in a single tap, and no precision gestures.
3. Never lose an answer: drafts persist locally until they are saved or cleared.
4. Every check is phrased so that the answer is obvious, so the buyer never wonders what "yes" means.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.2 AA. Every control has an accessible name, the tap targets meet the minimum size, and the layout must stay readable in bright daylight on a phone screen.
